Abstract

Enterprise workflow systems route millions of tasks daily across heterogeneous processing nodes, yet most routing policies rely on static rules or simple heuristics that fail to adapt to shifting workloads. We introduce FlowNet, a reinforcement-learning framework that learns adaptive routing policies for enterprise workflow orchestration. FlowNet models the workflow graph as a Markov Decision Process where states encode queue depths, node capacities, and task metadata, and actions correspond to routing decisions at each junction. Using a novel reward signal derived from end-to-end latency, throughput, and SLA compliance, our agent learns policies that dynamically balance load across processing stages. We evaluate FlowNet on MeridianFlow, a production workflow engine serving 2.3 million daily tasks at Meridian Systems, and on two open-source benchmarks (Apache Airflow traces and Temporal.io replay logs). FlowNet reduces median task latency by 34%, improves SLA compliance from 91.2% to 98.7%, and increases peak throughput by 22% compared to the best rule-based baseline. Ablation studies reveal that the temporal attention mechanism and hierarchical action space are critical to performance. Our approach demonstrates that RL-based optimization can deliver significant operational improvements in real-world enterprise workflow systems.
